# Housing and major purchases

These original learning articles use general principles. Property, lending, tenancy, and consumer laws vary by location.

## Article 36: Renting versus buying a home
Renting and buying involve different costs, flexibility, and risks. Ownership costs can include a down payment, loan interest, taxes, insurance, maintenance, transaction costs, and periods when the property is hard to sell. Renters may have fewer repair responsibilities but less control over renewal and future rent. Compare the full cost over the expected time in the home and consider mobility, savings, local conditions, and personal priorities.

## Article 37: Understanding a mortgage
A mortgage is a loan secured by property. Compare the interest rate, whether it can change, repayment term, fees, required insurance, closing costs, and total payments. A lower initial payment may reflect a temporary rate or other conditions. A lender's maximum approval is not necessarily a comfortable budget. Keep room for maintenance and other housing costs, and read the loan documents before signing.

## Article 38: The full cost of vehicle ownership
A vehicle's cost includes more than its purchase price or monthly loan payment. Consider financing interest, insurance, fuel or charging, maintenance, registration, taxes, parking, and expected depreciation. A longer loan can make the payment appear affordable while increasing interest and extending the period of debt. Compare the total amount paid and ensure the loan term does not outlast the expected useful life of the vehicle.

## Article 39: Comparing education financing
Education funding may come from savings, grants, scholarships, work, or loans, each with different conditions. Compare total program cost, likely completion time, employment outcomes, loan rates and terms, and repayment obligations. Borrowing rules and forgiveness programs are jurisdiction-specific and may change. Verify offers with official sources, avoid relying only on projected salary, and understand who is legally responsible for each loan.

## Article 40: Preparing for a large purchase
Before a major purchase, define the need, set a total-cost limit, and compare options using the same assumptions. Include delivery, setup, maintenance, financing, insurance, and resale value when relevant. Waiting can create time to compare prices and rebuild savings, but urgency or limited-time offers can pressure decisions. Read cancellation and return terms, verify the seller, and avoid financing a purchase until its full repayment terms are clear.
