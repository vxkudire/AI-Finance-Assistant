# Consumer protection and financial decision-making

These original learning articles focus on general safeguards. Consumer rights, regulated roles, and reporting channels differ by jurisdiction.

## Article 46: Recognizing financial scams
Scams often use urgency, secrecy, guaranteed returns, impersonation, or requests to pay through hard-to-reverse methods. Pause before sending money or sharing information. Independently verify an organization using contact details from an official source, not a link or number supplied by the requester. Be skeptical of unsolicited investment opportunities and pressure to recruit others. Report suspected fraud through the appropriate local authorities and contact your financial institution promptly if money or credentials were exposed.

## Article 47: Protecting financial accounts
Use unique passwords, multifactor authentication where available, and alerts for account activity. Never share one-time authentication codes with someone who contacts you unexpectedly. Review statements and credit reports through official channels and report unfamiliar transactions quickly. Be cautious with links, attachments, public Wi-Fi, and callers claiming to be financial institutions. Security steps reduce some risks but cannot guarantee that an account or identity will never be compromised.

## Article 48: Choosing a financial professional
Before hiring a financial professional, verify credentials, registration, services, compensation, conflicts of interest, and disciplinary history through official local sources. Ask whether fees are hourly, flat, asset-based, commission-based, or a combination, and request the details in writing. Titles and legal duties vary across jurisdictions. Make sure the professional's role matches the task, and do not share passwords or sign documents you do not understand.

## Article 49: Behavioral biases and money
People may overreact to recent market news, avoid acknowledging losses, follow a crowd, or favor familiar investments. These tendencies can lead to rushed decisions or concentrated risk. A written plan, waiting period for major changes, and periodic review can help create consistency. The goal is not to eliminate emotion but to notice when it is influencing a decision. A plan should still be updated when facts or personal circumstances genuinely change.

## Article 50: Making a sound financial decision
For a consequential choice, define the goal, compare realistic alternatives, identify worst-case costs, and check how each option affects other priorities. Verify assumptions, fees, reversibility, and deadlines using primary documents. Consider asking an independent qualified professional when the consequences are large or rules are complex. Avoid deciding under pressure, and keep a record of what you chose and why so the choice can be reviewed later.
