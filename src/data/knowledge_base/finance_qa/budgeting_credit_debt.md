# Budgeting, credit, and debt

These original learning articles describe general decision frameworks. Credit systems, consumer protections, and loan terms vary by jurisdiction and provider.

## Article 6: Emergency reserves
An emergency reserve is accessible money for unplanned costs or interruptions in income. The appropriate amount depends on essential expenses, job stability, insurance, dependents, and access to other resources. A common approach is to estimate several months of essential costs, then build the reserve gradually. Keep emergency money in a place that is relatively stable and accessible, while checking account protections, withdrawal limits, and fees.

## Article 7: Planning for irregular expenses
Annual premiums, repairs, school costs, and seasonal bills are predictable even if they do not arrive monthly. List these costs, estimate their timing and amount, and divide the needed sum across the months before payment. Holding these contributions in a separate savings category can prevent a known bill from being mistaken for an emergency. Estimates will be imperfect; update them when actual bills arrive.

## Article 8: How credit scores work
Credit scores summarize information in a credit file using a scoring model. Factors can include payment history, balances relative to available limits, account age, and recent applications, but the model and weighting vary. A score is not a complete measure of financial well-being and does not guarantee approval or a specific rate. Review credit reports for accuracy, pay obligations on time when possible, and use official local processes to dispute errors.

## Article 9: Comparing borrowing costs
The interest rate is only one part of a loan's cost. Compare the annual percentage rate or equivalent disclosure, fees, repayment term, payment schedule, variable-rate conditions, and total amount repaid. A longer term may lower each payment while increasing total interest or keeping the borrower indebted longer. Ask how missed payments, early repayment, and rate changes are handled before accepting an offer.

## Article 10: Choosing a debt repayment strategy
Two common repayment approaches are directing extra money to the highest-rate balance first or clearing the smallest balance first for visible progress. The highest-rate approach can reduce interest cost when all required payments are made; the smallest-balance approach may help some people stay motivated. Neither replaces a plan for minimum payments, essential expenses, and emergency needs. Compare fees and consequences before consolidating or refinancing debt.
