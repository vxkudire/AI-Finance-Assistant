# Insurance and personal risk

These original articles explain general risk-management concepts, not coverage recommendations. Policy language, legal duties, and public coverage differ by location.

## Article 31: What insurance does
Insurance transfers specified financial risks to an insurer in exchange for premiums, subject to policy terms. It can help protect against a loss that would be difficult to absorb, but it does not cover every event or cost. Compare covered events, exclusions, deductibles, limits, waiting periods, claims procedures, and insurer reliability. Choose coverage based on potential financial impact and existing protections, not just the premium.

## Article 32: Deductibles and coverage limits
A deductible is an amount the policyholder may have to pay before some coverage applies; a limit caps what the insurer will pay for covered losses. A higher deductible can lower a premium but requires enough available funds to handle a claim. Limits and exclusions can leave substantial costs uncovered. Review the full policy wording and confirm whether separate limits apply to specific events or types of property.

## Article 33: Health-cost planning
Healthcare costs can include premiums, deductibles, copayments, uncovered services, and time away from work. Coverage options and public programs vary by country and circumstance. Compare expected use, provider networks, medicines, out-of-pocket limits, and enrollment rules—not only monthly premiums. Keep personal medical and financial records private, and consult official program materials for eligibility and deadlines.

## Article 34: Life insurance basics
Life insurance may provide financial support to beneficiaries after the insured person's death, if the policy is active and the claim meets its terms. The need and amount depend on obligations such as dependents, shared debts, and future income needs. Policies differ in duration, premiums, cash values, exclusions, and cancellation terms. Compare the guaranteed and non-guaranteed elements carefully and avoid buying a policy based only on a sales illustration.

## Article 35: Disability and income interruption
Disability coverage may replace part of income when illness or injury prevents work, subject to eligibility and policy definitions. Important details include the waiting period, benefit duration, definition of disability, offsets, exclusions, and whether premiums or benefits are taxable locally. Emergency savings, paid leave, public benefits, and employer coverage may also matter. Review these sources together so that assumptions about income protection are realistic.
