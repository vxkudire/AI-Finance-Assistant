# Retirement and long-term planning

These original learning articles cover general planning ideas. Retirement systems, account rules, and public benefits vary substantially by country and may change.

## Article 26: Starting a retirement plan
Retirement planning begins with estimating future spending, identifying likely income sources, and considering when work may change or stop. Estimates are uncertain: health, housing, inflation, investment returns, and lifespan all affect the result. A practical plan can be reviewed and adjusted rather than treated as a fixed prediction. Learn the rules and costs of available savings accounts before deciding how much to contribute or where to invest.

## Article 27: Employer retirement benefits
Some employers offer retirement contributions, matching, or other benefits. Read the plan's eligibility, matching formula, vesting schedule, investment choices, fees, and withdrawal rules. A match can be valuable compensation, but details matter: contributions may not vest immediately, and access may be restricted. Compare the plan with other financial priorities and confirm benefit terms with official plan documents or the plan administrator.

## Article 28: Public retirement benefits
Government retirement benefits may depend on work history, contributions, age, residence, or other eligibility rules. Benefit estimates are projections and can change with future earnings or policy. Use official government statements and calculators rather than relying on informal estimates. Consider public benefits as one part of a broader plan alongside savings, employment income, housing, and health costs; do not assume one program will cover every future expense.

## Article 29: Planning withdrawals from savings
Retirement withdrawals must balance current spending against the risk of outliving savings. A fixed withdrawal percentage is only a rule of thumb, not a guarantee, because returns, inflation, taxes, fees, and lifespan vary. A poor sequence of returns early in retirement can have a lasting effect when withdrawals continue. Review spending flexibility, account rules, cash needs, and the portfolio regularly, and stress-test a plan under less favorable assumptions.

## Article 30: Beneficiaries and account continuity
Financial accounts and insurance policies may allow named beneficiaries, and those designations can affect who receives assets. Keep designations current after major life changes and confirm how primary and contingent beneficiaries work. A will may not override every account designation, and rules differ by jurisdiction. Coordinate records with an estate-planning professional when needed, and store account and contact information securely so trusted people can locate it.
