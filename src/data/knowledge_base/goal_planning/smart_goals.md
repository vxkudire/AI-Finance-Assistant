# Financial goal planning
SMART goals are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. Example: "Save a $40,000 house down payment in 5 years."
Shorter-horizon goals (under about 3-5 years) usually favour lower-volatility holdings because there is little time to recover from a downturn; long-horizon goals such as retirement can tolerate more volatility.
The required monthly saving depends on three levers: the time available, the amount contributed, and the assumed investment return. Increasing contributions is the lever fully in the saver's control.
Retirement planning commonly considers expected expenses, Social Security, inflation, and sequence-of-returns risk.
