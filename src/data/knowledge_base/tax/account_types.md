# US account types (concepts)
A traditional 401(k) or IRA is funded with pre-tax (or deductible) dollars; money grows tax-deferred and withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.
A Roth 401(k) or Roth IRA is funded with after-tax dollars; qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. Eligibility and contribution limits are set by the IRS and change over time; check irs.gov.
An HSA (Health Savings Account) offers tax advantages when paired with an eligible high-deductible health plan.
A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged account for education expenses.
Employer matching contributions in a 401(k) are part of compensation and are commonly described as a very high-value benefit.
